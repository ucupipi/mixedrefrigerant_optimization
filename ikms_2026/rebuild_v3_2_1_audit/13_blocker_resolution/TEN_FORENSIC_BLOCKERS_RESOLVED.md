Because some tables in the earlier report were not re-generated, the old figure (11.20 GWh/yr) persisted in historical audit comparisons while 13.92 GWh/yr was published in the executive ledger.

## 7. BLOCKER 7: CANDIDATE R 4.6% MITA RISK & STATISTICAL POWER

### 7.1 Analytical Formulation
- Minimum temperature approach threshold: $\text{MITA}_{\text{min}} = 1.500^\circ\text{C}$
- Candidate R nominal MITA: $\mu = 2.550^\circ\text{C}$
- Combined standard uncertainty (GUM): $u_c = 0.622^\circ\text{C}$
- Standardized $z$-score:
  $$z = \frac{1.500 - 2.550}{0.622} = -1.6881$$
- Analytical Gaussian cumulative probability:
  $$P(\text{MITA} < 1.500^\circ\text{C}) = \Phi(-1.6881) = \mathbf{4.57\%}$$

### 7.2 Monte Carlo Sample Size and Confidence Interval
For $N = 1,000$ draws, expected violations $k = 46$ ($4.6\%$).
The exact **Clopper-Pearson 95% Confidence Interval** is:
$$\text{CI}_{95\%} = [3.39\%, \mathbf{6.09\%}]$$
Because the upper bound of the confidence interval ($6.09\%$) exceeds the $5.0\%$ engineering threshold, an empirical test with $N = 1,000$ **fails to reject the null hypothesis $H_0: P_{\text{viol}} \ge 5.0\%$**.

To statistically prove $P < 5.0\%$ with a margin of error $E = \pm 0.5\%$ at $95\%$ confidence:
$$N \ge \frac{z_{0.975}^2 \cdot p(1-p)}{E^2} = \frac{1.96^2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95}{0.005^2} = \mathbf{7,299\text{ draws}}$$
